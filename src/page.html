Research focus

Models a clinician can audit

Medical AI earns trust when a clinician can see why it decided and can fix it when it is wrong. I study models whose reasoning passes through clinical concepts, and I test whether those concepts carry only what their names say.

Concept-based models for physiological signals

Concept bottleneck and concept embedding models for the ECG, with intervention tests that measure whether a clinician's correction improves the diagnosis.

Concept leakage and model audits

How concept scores smuggle label information past a bottleneck, and the audits that catch it before a result reaches a paper.

Grounded clinical language generation

Language models that write only what a structured evidence record supports, scored sentence by sentence for unsupported findings, contradictions, and coverage.

Calibration and clinical evaluation

Calibrated probabilities, operating points fixed on validation data, multi-seed comparisons, and confidence intervals that let a cardiologist weigh a result.

MS thesis · 2025–2026

Explainable valvular heart disease diagnosis from the 12-lead ECG

Supervisor Dr. Nazia Perwaiz, NUST SEECS Data EchoNext, Columbia University Irving Medical Center (PhysioNet) Compute PyTorch on Colab RTX PRO 6000 GPUs

Echocardiography confirms valve disease, but it needs an ultrasound machine, a sonographer, and a cardiologist. The 12-lead ECG takes seconds and is available in most clinics. Deep networks can detect valve disease from the ECG, but they return a score without a reason a cardiologist can check.

My thesis places named clinical concepts between the signal and the diagnosis. A clinician can read each concept, overwrite it with a measured value, and watch the diagnosis update. A language model then turns the concepts into a report, and a checker compares every sentence with the evidence it received.

→ → → →

Each record pairs a 12-lead ECG with an echocardiogram from the same patient. I split by patient, so no person appears in two splits, and the validation and test sets keep one ECG per patient.

Signal
12 leads, 10 s at 250 Hz (2,500 samples per lead), zero-phase band-pass 0.05–40 Hz
Tabular
Sex, age, ventricular rate, atrial rate, PR interval, QRS duration, QTc
Splits
72,475 training ECGs; 4,626 validation and 5,442 test patients
Labels
Moderate or greater AS, AR, MR, and TR from echocardiography reports

A 1D residual network reads the waveform, a small MLP reads the tabular values, and the two join into one 576-dimensional vector. Every model family in the comparison shares this encoder, so differences come from the head and the training scheme alone.

Waveform
ResNet1d-18: stem, four residual stages from 64 to 512 channels, global average pooling
Tabular
MLP 7 → 128 → 64 with batch normalisation and dropout
Training
Focal loss, AdamW, cosine schedule, bf16, early stopping on validation AUROC
Seeds
Five per model family, with seed-paired comparisons

None of the ten concepts uses a valve label. Where an echocardiogram lacks a measurement, the concept is masked from the loss, so a missing value never counts as a healthy one.

The primary model is a concept embedding model. Each concept owns two learned 16-dimensional embeddings, one for present and one for absent, mixed by the concept's probability. During training, a quarter of the measured concepts switch to their true values at random, so the model learns to use a clinician's correction.

Outputs
Probabilities for moderate or greater AS, AR, MR, and TR; a five-output variant adds composite structural heart disease
Selection
Highest five-seed AUROC among concept models within 5 points of the black box whose accuracy does not drop under correction; rule fixed before the final runs
Calibration
Platt scaling fitted on validation data; thresholds at 90% specificity chosen on validation

The language model never sees the waveform or the network's hidden state. It reads one structured record, the Evidence Token, and a word-boundary checker scores its report for fabricated findings, contradictions of absent concepts, and missing findings.

What the experiments show

  • Accuracy

    Across five seeds, the concept embedding model (CEM) reached mean AUROC 0.827 ± 0.005 against 0.821 ± 0.004 for a black box with the same encoder, and scored higher in all five seed pairs.

  • Clinician correction

    Measured echo values in place of predicted concepts raised CEM accuracy by 1.73 ± 0.21 AUROC points. Joint and sequential bottleneck models lost 5.75 and 6.91 points under the same correction.

  • Hidden leakage

    A sequential model, whose concept stage never saw a valve label, still reached aortic stenosis AUROC 0.821 from predicted concepts, above the 0.754 reached from the true concepts. Soft concept scores carry label information that the concept names do not describe.

  • Screening point

    At 90% specificity, fixed on validation data, the five-seed CEM ensemble found 61.5% of moderate or greater aortic stenosis with a negative predictive value of 0.977.

  • Structural disease

    One model predicts the four valve lesions and composite structural heart disease, with AUROC 0.841 against 0.831 for the black box (DeLong p = 1.0 × 10−5).

  • Report safety

    Under prompt instructions alone, three medical LLMs put out-of-evidence terms in 14%–40% of reports on 100 test cases. The Evidence Token template produced none across 5,442 reports. Current work compares MedGemma, BioMistral, and a general LLM with sentence-level checks and LoRA fine-tuning.

Research practice

Audit before claims

Before the final experiments, I audited my own first pipeline line by line. Each problem changed what the thesis may claim, and each fix is in the results above.

Target leakage in the first concept set

Four of 15 concepts came from the valve labels; the right atrial enlargement label alone reached AUROC 1.000 for tricuspid regurgitation.

I rebuilt the concepts from echo measurements that exclude every valve label, with missing measurements masked from the loss.

Filter mismatch between training and evaluation

Training used a 0.05–40 Hz band-pass, but evaluation used 0.5–40 Hz.

Re-evaluation at the training band gave mean AUROC 0.815 against 0.816, so the reported accuracy held.

A substring bug in the hallucination checker

The checker matched substrings, so the arrhythmia term “pac” counted inside “impact”.

Word-boundary matching lowered one model's rate from 35% to 29%; every report metric now uses it.

Choices made after seeing results

A model, threshold, or calibration chosen on the test set inflates results.

I fixed the model-selection rule before the five-seed runs, and chose thresholds and calibration on validation data only.
